    Every number on this site, explained in plain English

    Weather sites throw words at you — “surface pressure”, “CAPE”, “aerosol optical depth” — and never say what they mean. This page does. Two more were added afterwards and sit in their own group at the end, so the original list stays intact. Read it once, then head to the forecast.

    Rain and snow
    Precipitation

    How much rain, snow or hail is expected, and how likely it is. One millimetre is one litre of water on every square metre of ground, so 10 mm is a good soaking.

    🌾
    Pollen
    Pollen concentration

    How much pollen is expected in the air, split into trees, grass and weeds, with a level from low to very high. The numbers are grains per cubic metre, and each type is judged on its own scale because birch and grass are counted differently.

    Regional, not worldwide: the model covers Europe and returns nothing for most of Asia, Africa, the Americas and Oceania. It is also modelled rather than measured, on a grid of roughly 40 km. Where there is no coverage the site says so instead of showing a zero.

    The plumbing

    Where these numbers actually come from

    Nothing here is typed in by hand, and nothing is generated by an AI. Every value on the forecast page is fetched from a public weather API when you open the page, and the page says how old it is.

    1

    You pick a place

    Search by city name or use your location. The name is turned into coordinates once, and those coordinates are what every request uses.

    2

    The server asks the API

    A small proxy fetches from Open-Meteo and caches the reply for ten minutes. Caching is what keeps the site inside the free tier — one visitor does not mean one upstream call.

    3

    The page renders it

    Each card reads its own field. If a field is missing, the card says so instead of showing a zero. There is no invented fallback anywhere on the page.

    The forecast models behind the numbers are the national ones — ECMWF, GFS, ICON and others — blended by Open-Meteo. This site does not run its own model and does not claim to.

    Rules this site follows

    What we will not fake

    Most of the trust problems on weather sites come from showing a confident number that was never measured. These are the specific cases, and what happens instead.

    🌍 Global values get a global badgeCO₂ is measured near cities, not at your window. It carries a “not local weather” label rather than being dressed up as a local reading.
    🛰️ Satellite data says satelliteGround temperature comes from a model grid, not a thermometer in your street. The card says “estimated” and explains which estimate it is.
    🌊 Sea data disappears inlandNear the coast you get wave height and sea temperature. Inland, the card is removed entirely instead of showing a blank box that looks broken.
    🌙 Polar day and night are namedAt high latitudes the sun does not always rise. The page says “no sunset — midnight sun” or “polar night” instead of printing 12:00 AM.
    ☀️ Sunshine is a whole-day totalThe sunshine figure is a forecast for the entire day, so it does not drop to zero at noon. The label says so.
    💧 Evaporation is a rate, not a totalWater leaving the soil is quoted in millimetres per hour. Multiplying it by 24 to get a daily figure would be wrong, so we do not.
    📉 Missing means missingWhen a value is unavailable the card shows a dash and a reason. We never substitute a plausible-looking number to keep the layout tidy.
    🏷️ Every number has a badgeLive, estimated, conditional, global, or background — each of the 25 features is labelled above, so you can tell at a glance how much weight to give it.
    Before you ask

    Questions people actually ask

    The short answers. Each one is about something a reader has genuinely questioned while using a weather site.

    Why does the site ask me to prove I am not a bot?

    The weather data is free but rate-limited. A single scraper in a loop can use up the day's allowance and break the site for everyone else. The check is a one-time speed bump, it takes about a second, and it happens once per visit rather than once per page.

    Be aware of what it is: a deterrent, not a lock. It stops casual scraping and bulk copying. It would not stop someone determined, because everything it does happens in your browser where it can be read. Real protection needs a server-side check such as Cloudflare Turnstile, and this project deliberately stays free and key-less instead.

    There is no account, no email, no cookie and no tracking pixel. Everything the check needs stays in your browser.

    How accurate is a forecast here?

    The accuracy is the underlying model's, not this site's. This page displays what Open-Meteo returns without adjusting it. In general, a three-day forecast is far more reliable than a seven-day one, and rainfall intensity is the least reliable field of all — the models agree on “rain likely” long before they agree on how much.

    A useful rule: trust the shape of the next 24 hours, treat anything past day five as a trend rather than a plan.

    Why is the temperature different from my phone's weather app?

    Different models, different grids, different update times. Your phone may be showing a reading from a station near you while this page shows an interpolated value for the grid cell containing your city — which can be several kilometres across.

    Urban heat is the other factor. A grid cell that averages a city with its surrounding countryside will read cooler than a thermometer on a hot street.

    What is CAPE, and why is the storm score a percentage?

    CAPE measures how much energy a rising parcel of air has available — high values mean the atmosphere is primed for thunderstorms. The raw unit (joules per kilogram) is not intuitive, so the storm card converts it to a 0–100 score and labels the result.

    It is an indicator, not a warning service. For official thunderstorm warnings, follow your national meteorological agency.

    Does this work offline, or as an app?

    No. Every value is fetched live, so with no connection there is nothing to show and the page tells you so with a retry button. It is a website, not an installed app, and it does not claim to work offline.

    Is any of this paid?

    No. The weather, air-quality, marine and geocoding data all come from Open-Meteo's free tier, and the map outlines come from OpenStreetMap volunteers. There is no API key in this project and no billing account behind it.

    The trade-off is the free tier's limits: it is intended for non-commercial use, with a rough ceiling of about ten thousand requests a day. If this ever became popular enough to exceed that, the honest options are to pay, to cache harder, or to slow down — not to quietly start scraping someone else.

    Why do only 25 features appear when there are more weather variables than that?

    Because the other thirty on the official climate list need satellites or yearly reports rather than a live forecast. Showing them would mean showing something that is not actually being measured, so they are deliberately left out — and the note at the bottom of this page names that decision rather than hiding it.
